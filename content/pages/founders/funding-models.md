# The funding models, from open source to proprietary

How a company licenses what it builds decides what it can sell, what an investor is buying, and what the founder keeps if things go wrong. These are the main models, from the most open to the most closed. The sgit project uses the first one.

| Model | What is open | How it earns | What the founder gets | What the investor gets | The risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open source, all of it** | The whole product, under a licence such as Apache-2.0 | Hosting, support, services, a managed version, a meter on use | Keeps the right to use the work wherever they go; adoption without a sales team | A company whose moat is execution and trust, not code | Someone else can host it; revenue has to come from being the best operator |
| **Open core** | The core; enterprise features stay closed | Licences for the closed features, often per seat | Adoption from the open part, revenue from the closed part | A familiar software business with a community funnel | The line between open and closed is argued in every release, and tends to move towards lock-in |
| **Source-available** | The code is readable, under a licence that limits commercial use | Licences, often to stop clouds hosting it | Some of the trust of open source, some protection from competitors | Protection against a large host copying the product | It is not open source, and contributors and customers know it |
| **Proprietary** | Nothing | Licences and subscriptions | Full control of the product | The classic software model, valued on revenue | Customers must trust what they cannot inspect; the founder loses the work if they leave |

## Three arguments worth reading

- **[Open source what you build, even while you are employed](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/open-source-so-you-keep-it.html).** Nobody copies your open source ideas; the real advantage is that you keep using your own work from job to job.
- **[Sell, don't issue](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/sell-dont-issue.html).** For founders who have already won: the investor buys the founder's shares, the founder lends the company what it needs, and open source keeps everyone honest.
- **[A small slice sets the price](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/a-small-slice-sets-the-price.html).** The 2026 mega-listings priced very large companies by selling a few per cent of them, and the founder's lesson from that.

The [evaluation framework](/framework/index.html#open-source) asks the investor's version of the same question: is the open source strategy real, or lock-in? Its test: "Can a motivated team take the open source project and, without purchasing anything, solve a real problem end-to-end?"

## sgit's own choice

sgit is open source. Where investment in it should go, the tool, a hub for vaults, or open core, is argued in [where to invest in sgit](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/where-to-invest-in-sgit.html), and is the start of [the case study](/case-study/index.html).

## What this does not claim

- **Four rows simplify a spectrum.** Real companies mix models, and licences differ in ways that matter.
- **The table is a position, not a measurement.** It is how we see the trade-offs, with the arguments linked.
