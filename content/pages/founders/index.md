# For founders: talking to an investor, and what comes next

The aim of this section is that a founder's life gets easier: knowing what an investor is listening for, what the next steps are, what to have ready, and what help exists. It is written for the first conversations, angel to Series A.

## Talking to an investor

- **Say what the company does in one sentence, then stop.** The second sentence should come from their question, not your script.
- **Lead with the evidence, not the vision.** Users, revenue, a pilot that renewed, a waiting list: one real number beats a market size.
- **Know why now.** Investors ask what changed that makes this possible or necessary today and not five years ago.
- **Know your round.** How much, for what, for how long, and what milestone it buys. A round is priced against the next round's story.
- **Ask about them.** Their fund's stage, cheque size, how decisions are made, who else must meet you, and what they have backed that is like you. A no from the wrong fund is information, not failure.
- **Expect to be read as a person.** At this stage investors are judging the founder as much as the company; [the controllable founder](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-controllable-founder.html) is one view of what they look for, and of what it can cost.

## The steps of a round

A typical early round runs in this order. Every fund varies it, and the steps overlap.

| # | Step | What happens | What to have ready |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction | A warm introduction, usually from a founder they backed or a mutual contact | A short note and a deck that reads without you |
| 2 | First meeting | Thirty to sixty minutes on the problem, the team and the evidence | Your numbers, and your answer to "why now" |
| 3 | Follow-ups | More of the team meets you; they call customers and references | A data room: the documents they will ask for, in one place |
| 4 | Partner or committee meeting | The fund decides whether to offer terms | A clear ask: amount, use of funds, milestones |
| 5 | Term sheet | A short, mostly non-binding summary of the deal | Advice from a lawyer who does venture rounds; see [one term sheet, clause by clause](/contracts/term-sheet.html) |
| 6 | Due diligence | Legal, financial and technical checks on what you said | Clean cap table, signed IP assignments, contracts, accounts |
| 7 | Long-form documents | The subscription and shareholders' agreement, new articles, the disclosure letter | Time: this is where weeks go; see [what the term sheet becomes](/contracts/index.html) |
| 8 | Completion | Money arrives, shares are issued, the register is updated | Board minutes, filings, the new cap table |

## What to prepare, and where to keep it

Steps 3 and 6 are where the paperwork lives: the deck, the financial model, the cap table, the incorporation documents, IP assignments, key contracts and the founders' agreements. Most founders keep these in a shared folder whose access nobody can list a month later.

The proposal in [deal rooms as vaults](/deal-rooms/index.html) is to keep them in a vault instead: encrypted so the host cannot read it, shared by key, with a record of what was shared, when, and with whom.

## What help exists

- **Angel networks and syndicates,** which pool angels' time for due diligence and often their cheques.
- **Accelerators,** which trade a small stake for a programme, a network and often a first cheque.
- **Tax-advantaged schemes,** in the UK the Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme and the Enterprise Investment Scheme, which give investors tax relief and so make a round easier to fill. Read [GOV.UK on SEIS](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/seed-enterprise-investment-scheme-background), and apply for [advance assurance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/venture-capital-schemes-apply-for-advance-assurance) before the round, since many investors ask for it.
- **Employee options,** which in the UK are often granted as [EMI options](https://www.gov.uk/tax-employee-share-schemes/enterprise-management-incentives-emis) where the company qualifies.
- **Lawyers and accountants who do venture work.** The cost of a bad clause is far larger than the fee; see [legal on demand for startups](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/legal-on-demand-for-startups.html) for how that work could be priced differently.
- **Before all of it, the company.** [Start a company](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/start-a-company.html) covers the UK, Portugal and Lithuania.

## What this does not claim

- **The steps are typical, not universal.** Funds, countries and stages differ, and some rounds run in a fortnight.
- **The schemes change.** Their rules and limits are set by government and updated; check the source before relying on them.
- **Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.**
